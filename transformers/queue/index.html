M/M/c · one line or a line each Transformer Learning

A store with several registers. People arrive at rate λ, and each register, while someone is there, checks them out at rate μ. Utilization ρ = λ/(cμ) is the fraction of time each register is busy. It is the same whether there is one line or a line at each register. The wait is not. A line at each register is c separate M/M/1 queues: you stay in the line you walked up to, so a register can sit idle while another line still has people. One line feeding every register is an M/M/c queue: the next free register takes the next person. Click a symbol. The store marks the thing that symbol names. On one contended resource the useful load is 60–70%. The bar under ρ marks that band.

The rates are averages

Arrivals and checkouts are messy. λ is how many people arrive per unit time, on average, so the mean gap is 1/λ. μ is how many checkouts one register finishes per unit time, on average, so the mean checkout is 1/μ. The bars are this store. The curve is the exponential those averages imply. The dash is the mean.

Why 60–70% is the load

The resource under contention is whatever is doing the work. In this store it is a register. ρ is the share of its time already busy, and 1−ρ is the slack. Arrivals and checkouts are random, so a burst clears only as fast as that slack. One register’s mean wait is ρ/(μ(1−ρ)): the load divided by the slack, counted in checkouts. The table is that ratio. Under the band the wait is already short, and more idle time buys little. Through the band the ratio stays small. Past the band each step of load costs more than the one before, because the slack in the denominator is shrinking. A line at each register is one resource, so it wants this band. One shared line pools the slack, so its curve stays low further right.
